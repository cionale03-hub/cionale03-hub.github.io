Rebuilding CareLinx's VA Care-to-Cash Lifecycle

From referral intake to paid claim, across two VA payers

Role: Product Manager
Company: CareLinx
Domain: VA home care, revenue cycle
Partners: Ops, Payments, Engineering

The problem

VA home care runs on referrals authorizing fixed weekly hours for Respite and Home Health Aide care. Hours change mid-period, expire after 180 days, and often need extensions while a new authorization is pending. Intake, voucher balances, shift booking, and claims ran separately, so authorized hours, delivered care, and billed claims drifted apart, causing denials and revenue at risk.

What I did

  • Mapped the full flow from referral to paid claim with operations, payments, and engineering.
  • Designed a referral history model with one active authorization per member per program, with validation blocking overlapping, expired, or incomplete referrals.
  • Specified automated voucher logic keeping balances in sync with approved hours as authorizations change.
  • Added shift controls blocking care scheduled outside the current workweek or past authorization expiry.
  • Designed a 90-day extension path so care continued and billing didn't stall while new authorizations were pending.
  • Planned migration of ~584 existing VA families and scoped a second payer launch.
  • Ran monthly governance reviews of denials, resubmissions, and pending authorizations with ops and leadership.

Results

  • Grew monthly finalized VA payments 64% over seven months, from $2.7M to $4.4M.
  • Held the claim denial rate at 2–3% from March through August, down from 5.5% in February.
  • Processed ~$26M in finalized claims through the automated pipeline (Feb–Aug).
  • Revenue exposure after resubmissions reduced to ~$790K.
  • Kept 95%+ of $621K in pending-authorization revenue within the 90-day conversion window.

The insight that changed the strategy

In July, denials spiked to 5%. I traced it to authorization delays in one regional VA network; denials returned to 2.5% the next month once those were cleared.

In August, I found that 70% of renewed authorizations didn't take effect for 1–2 months, leaving already-delivered care uncovered: 2,213 hours, $82K, ~40% of all uncovered extension cost.

I brought it to leadership with a recommendation to pursue retroactive authorization with the VA and stand up a dedicated recovery process.
